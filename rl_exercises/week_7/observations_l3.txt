The DQN ensemble requires more training time due to the use of multiple actors 
and multiple Q-network heads. As a result, I was unable to conduct extensive hyperparameter or 
multi-seed experiments.
The results from a single training run (see plots/Level_3_training_curves.png) 
indicate that the DQN ensemble achieves better sample efficiency compared to 
the rnd_dqn, in the sense that it requires fewer environment steps 
to reach comparable reward levels. However, training for 100,000 steps is significantly 
more computationally expensive, which could potentially be mitigated through improved 
parallelization.
In the early phase of training (approximately the first 50,000 steps), 
both methods show similar performance and learning speed. The advantages of the ensemble 
approach become more apparent in the later phase, where the DQN ensemble continues to improve 
more consistently, while the rnd_dqn tends to plateau earlier.
This suggests that the ensemble-based exploration mechanism improves long-term exploration 
behavior rather than immediate learning speed (focus on parts of uncertainty where improvement is 
still possivle). In early training there are many possibilities to find better policies by random 
exploration and function approximation noise, where both methods behave similarly. Over time, 
the epistemic uncertainty estimates provided by the ensemble appear to help the agent escape 
suboptimal policies and sustain improvement, suggesting a more robust exploration mechanism 
compared to simply adding an intrinsic reward signal as used in the RND-based approach.
